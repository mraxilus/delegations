The state space

Every frame two facing bodies can hold, hand to hand

A frame is what each hand of the lead holds, and which arm lies on top where the forearms overlap. Two laws close it. One hand of the lead holds a hand of the follow at most once, and an arm order is recorded exactly where the forearms overlap. That is the whole state space.

{{gallery}}
lead’s Left arm lead’s Right arm Seen from above · lead at the bottom in squares, follow at the top in circles, each with a chevron for the way they face · a connection goes round a body rather than through one, so a crossed hold is drawn crossing

The model derives a crossing rather than lists it. Partners who face each other stand in mirror, so the Left of the lead faces the right of the follow. A connection runs parallel where it joins hands of opposite name, and it crosses where it joins hands of the same name. Two connections overlap only where both cross, which happens for one pair of holds.

So Left-to-left and Right-to-right carries an over and under distinction. Left-to-right and Right-to-left does not. Your sheet already has it that way, and the rule follows from who faces whom.

The shape of it

Every frame, and every move between them

Each row holds one more connection than the row below it, so the drawing says which way a move goes. A step up the page is a collect, and a step down is a drop. The tower is built upward, with free at its foot and both hands held at its head. A collect builds the frame up, and a drop lets it fall.

A line then says three things, and no more. The way up it runs says the direction. The word written on it names the hand of the follow that the move takes or lets go, in the plain shade of the follow. The ink of the line and the rest of the name say which arm of the lead acts, in the deep shade. So a name runs deep into plain, as the connection it makes does.

Where a name lies across its own line, the line is cut for it and rounded at both ends. So the break reads as a name rather than as a line that stops.

The two differ in one thing, which is whether the other arm stands in the way. A place has a clear path, so the hands overlap on the way past and the couple never quite lets go. A cut has no clear path, so the arm lets go and takes hold again on the far side of the arm in the way. That is why a cut is the one way between the two crossing orders.

1. There is no free, and it is {{free_moves}} of the {{moves}} moves

add a row

free is the frame with nothing held, which is four free hands, and that is where the name comes from. This ontology does not call it open, because you and every other dancer already use open position for the hand-to-hand frame. That frame is Left-to-right and Right-to-left here, so one word would have carried both.

Every single-hold state in your sheet has a drop that leads nowhere, because the sheet holds no frame with nothing connected. So the matrix does not close under drop, and the sheet cannot say that the couple let go.

It matters more now that a place is two moves rather than one. Every hand-off in your sheet is a drop and then a collect, which is all four pass cells. The frame between the two is free. So the sheet names four routes through a state that it does not hold.

Do: add one row and one column. Four collects out, four drops in, and the graph closes.

2. closed and half-closed are not hand positions

{{deferred}} cells depend on it

These two are the only states in your sheet that are not hand-to-hand, and the cells reaching them say what they are:

half-closed —slide→ Right to left
closed —slide→ Left-to-right and Right-to-left

A slide keeps contact, and contact travels along a body alone. No path runs from one hand of the follow to the other. So in both cells the right hand of the lead starts on the body of the follow. It slides down the arm it already touches to reach their left hand. That is the classic closed hold: the left hand to the right of the follow, and the right hand on their back.

Your vocabulary sheet reads them another way, with half-closed as Left to right and closed as Left-to-left and Right-to-right. That reading makes them duplicates of rows you already have, and it contradicts six of your own cells.

Do: hold them out until the body vocabulary lands with rotation. They are queued rather than lost, with trace, which waits on the same thing.

3. Two words have drifted between the sheets

naming

base uses slide and pass. Your vocabulary sheet defines trace and place, and never uses the other two. They are the same pairs, so each one needs one word.

Left-to-left around Right-to-right also sits in the same variant list as … over …. Over is part of the state, which is the arm on top. Around is a wrap, which is a modifier that rotation produces. While the two stay in one list, the position list grows every time an arm does something.

The three questions in the margins

Unsure if it is correct to either separate or combine these positions? I'm leaning heavily towards them being the same thing. Perhaps I need a clearer definition of what a frame position is.

One position, two states. Keep both readings.

A frame position is which hands hold what, such as Left-to-left and Right-to-right. It is what you call out on the floor. A frame is a position and the arm order together, and it is what the state machine moves between.

The test is whether a dancer passes between the two for free, and they cannot. A move from over-Left to over-Right takes a cut. Merge them, and the machine offers a move that the arms forbid. Name them apart everywhere, and you report a new position every time an arm changes height. In the code that costs one function: describe gives the state, and position gives the class.

Technically you can wrap around neck or torso… possibly can have lower and upper wrap as separate frame modifiers?

No. The level you already record does it.

Your own definitions say that a low lock sits behind the back, and that a high lock sits at the shoulder of the same arm. A low wrap crosses under the other arm, and a high wrap crosses over it. So the modifier and the level together give the place on the body, and nothing needs a name of its own:

Technically there's also Left-to-all and Right-to-all; I think I'll leave them out of this ontology.

Agreed, and the ambiguity cuts both ways.

One hand of the lead that holds both hands of the follow is one hand joined to two things. That is an ambiguous lead, and it would also make the state space uncountable. The law that rules it out is symmetric, so it rules out the mirror of it too. That mirror is two hands of the lead on one hand of the follow.

That second case is real. It is the moment a place passes through where the couple never let go. So the model routes a hand-off through free instead: let go, and take hold again. To keep contact through it is a quality of the lead's work, which is what your note means by a connection maintained through a trace. The frames on either side are the same either way.

Rotation

What follows for free, and what needs measuring

The twelve turn sheets are empty, so this is a proposal rather than a reading. A turn adds one quantity, which is twist: how far the body of the follow has turned against the body of the lead. It is one number for the couple rather than one for each arm. Both bodies are rigid, and both arms see the same relative rotation.

Two things that need no measurement at all

A rotation of the whole couple stores no twist. That is why a couple travel round the floor without unwinding. A model that added up the turns of each dancer on their own would be wrong.

The parity of the twist re-reads the whole base matrix. At half a turn the back of the follow is to the lead. Their left hand now stands on the left of the lead, and every connection that was crossed runs parallel. The frames do not change; the way they are read does. That is one function, not a second matrix.

Three numbers that do

One datum is available, which is your own: “if you're hand to hand you can only do 1 full rotation comfortably”. From it:

What joins the bodiesCapacityWhy
one hand-to-hand connection1 turn both dancers share the twist across two arms
two hand-to-hand connections½ turn the arms form a loop and bind
a hand on the bodynone the arm is already around the partner

Those three numbers already predict three things that match the floor. A wrap is led from a two-hand hold, and a lock from a one-hand hold. A turn out of closed position needs the back hand to leave first. A full turn on two hands is not comfortable.

The four cells worth dancing

Your rotations sheet has two filled cells, both for Left to left held low. Half a turn left gives a wrap, and a full turn right gives a lock.

Two rules fit both cells. Size alone says that one half turn wraps and two lock. The way round says that one way carries the arm across the front and wraps. The other carries it behind the back and locks, at either size. The two rules disagree about four cells that nobody has filled in:

left@0.5 magnitude → wrapdirection → wrap
left@1 magnitude → lockdirection → wrap
right@0.5 magnitude → wrapdirection → lock
right@1 magnitude → lockdirection → lock

All four are Left to left, held low. Dance them, and write down what the arm does. Then the twelve turn sheets stop being twelve matrices to fill in, and become one function of three numbers.

What is running

The model, the validator, and the laws

One state, one relation

A move exists between two frames exactly where one primitive is the difference between them. The names, the routes, the app and the audit all derive from that, so no table of moves is kept by hand.

A validator, not a toy

The browser app shows the frame you stand in, and every frame one move away. It shows every frame that is further, with how far away it is. Only what the app offers can be clicked, so a move that the ontology does not derive cannot be danced.
