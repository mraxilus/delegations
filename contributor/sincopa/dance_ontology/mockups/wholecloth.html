Whole cloth

Everything agreed so far, drawn again from the ground up. Six ideas follow, and each one needs the one before it. They are the marks, the heights and the holds. Then come the modifiers that an arm can wear, the turns that write them, and the moves between states.

This page keeps the conventions you accepted. They are the body as a circle with a chevron, and the two arm inks with a deep shade for the lead. Square stands against round, and the level fills stay. Everything else is derived again from your definitions, and marked as agreed, measured or proposed.

plate 1 · the marks

The conventions, kept exactly

These marks are settled, so every plate after this one draws in them and none restates them. A dancer is a circle with a chevron for the facing. A hand carries the ink of its own arm, which is blue for a left arm and orange for a right one. The lead wears the deep shade in squares, and the follow wears the plain shade in rounds. So the ink says which arm, and the shade and the shape say whose. The fill on a hand is a height, which the next plate defines.

a dancer: the chevron is the facing, and the rim is the body that the arms go round left arms: blue, deep and square for the lead, plain and round for the follow. A mark is the anchor of an arm, fixed at the side of the body, and it never moves right arms: orange, the same way round a connection: two arms that meet end to end. The rope in the colour of a mark is that arm, and its end is the hand. The seam where the two ropes meet is the joined hands, and the deep rope is always the lead's. A rope runs straight wherever it can, and it leaves a mark at the edge, so the fill stays readable faded: a free hand. Nobody holds it, and it keeps its own name. A free arm is never drawn, and only a held arm has a rope

plate 2 · the heights

A level is a height, and nothing else

Your definitions put the three levels on the body itself. Low is below the shoulder, about the torso. High is above the shoulder, about the neck. Above clears the head.

A level says where a connection rides, and it says nothing about which arm passes over which. Plate 4 says that, in the drawing. These pages are drawn from above the couple, so the fill carries the height.

Above is different in kind, and the drawing says so. A hand above sits over the head, inside the circle, on the axis that the couple turn about. That place is why plate 5 finds no limit on its turn, and why it can never wrap or lock.

plate 3 · the holds

Six holds, each at its own rest

A hold names which hands are joined, and nothing more. It is written lead to follow, and the case says who. Left to left is the Left of the lead that holds the left of the follow.

Every hold is drawn at its rest, which is the orientation where nothing is wound. For a cross-name hold that is face to face. For a same-name hold it is the follow turned away, where the connection runs clean down one side. At rest, every connection is a straight line.

Why the away rest matters. Drawn this way, Left to left at rest is the same shape as Left to right at rest. Only the facing of the follow differs, and with it which of their hands is held. So the two two-hand holds are one geometry, offset by that facing.

A half turn puts either pair in the X, with the follow away for the cross-name pair and facing for the same-name one. Neither X is a hold of its own. Counted from face to face, the away rest is @ −½.

The drawing decides which arm is over in the X. At the torso the sim finds the two arms of the X round opposite sides of the follow. One goes across the front, and one behind the back. So there is no crossing in plan for the drawing to settle.

Over the head the sim counts the crossings and names which arm is over. The break in the line that passes under is used between two connections only. Two arms of one body say which is over by their track and their fill, on the next plate.

plate 4 · the modifiers

What one arm can do, at its height

A modifier belongs to one arm of one dancer, either dancer, and it rides inside the name of that hand. Left-Lock-Low to right is the Left of the lead, locked low, that holds the right of the follow. Two drawing rules carry the anatomy.

An arm goes round the outside of the body circle, and it leaves its mark at the angle that lets it hug the rim. An above hand alone may sit inside, and never over the chevron. Only a held arm is drawn. So the height and the hug say whether an arm passes under or over the other arm of that dancer. Low and tight is under, and high and wide is over, and the free arm is never drawn to say it.

The mark never moves. The rope moves, and the hand is where the rope ends. A low lock ends just behind the opposite shoulder, and a wrap ends at the opposite front. A high lock comes back to its own shoulder as a small loopback at the mark, on the facing side. The elbow bends forward and never behind.

A loop of that kind is what an automaton draws where a state loops to itself. A connection is drawn whole, hand to hand, and an arm is never drawn alone. The turns count from face to face.

Each fact has one carrier. The track says lock or wrap, which is round the back or across the front. Where the rope ends says which lock it is, behind the opposite shoulder when low, and forward to its own shoulder when high. The fill on the fixed mark says the height, and with it under or over, which the width of the track repeats. Nothing else is written twice, so nothing can disagree. The turns in the captions count from face to face.

plate 5 · turning

Turning is what writes the modifiers

A position is a hold turned by a number of quarters, counted from face to face as your sheet counts. A same-name hold is drawn at its away rest, which sits half a turn along. The orientation is not decoration. Turn the held dancer, and the connection winds across their body, which is where a wrap and a lock come from. Your one worked row says it for Left to left held low. Half a turn one way is the wrap, and a whole turn the other way is the lock.

plate 6 · the moves

Two primitives, four named compounds

Everything that changes what is held comes down to two primitives. The four starred helpers earn their names, because a lead thinks of each one as a single action. A starred helper keeps contact through a trace, which is a hand that travels on the partner rather than through the air. These are definitions alone. No symbol is chosen for them yet.

collect primitive

Join a connection: a lead hand takes a follow hand.

drop primitive

Release it: the connection ends, both hands go free.

flick compound *

A drop with momentum: the lead sends the hand rather than lets it go.

trace compound *

Slide a held connection along the partner's body, and the contact holds all the way.

place compound *

Collect, then drop somewhere new, traced the whole way, with nothing in the path.

cut compound *

Drop, then collect past an arm, a torso or a neck in the way. The trace carries the contact over it.

The safety edge lives among these moves. Any move that makes a …-Lock-Low hand must come from a low carry. Collect low and turn, or trace down first. The state itself is safe, and one way into it is what the rule forbids.
